## 3. Positioning gaps

1. **More profiles on one phone.** Competitor family plans cap at 4 or 5 profiles; Sharp Ears supports 8. Matters for cousins and visiting children. Difficulty: low (shipped). Head start: Estimate: 3 to 6 months, because a competitor only has to raise a limit.
2. **A story world a child wants to return to, plus a grown-up course.** No competitor page I read describes a mascot-driven world. Difficulty: high (art, 31 painted poses and 3,500 recorded lines per facts). Head start: Estimate: 12 months, because it needs art and voice work that a competitor would have to commission.
3. **Nothing leaves the phone.** None of the pages I read states this. Difficulty: medium (the privacy claims need an audit under Families rules). Head start: Not found; it is a message, not a moat.

## 4. Threat assessment

1. **Simply Piano, high.** $50M raised, up to 5 profiles, child-age adaptation, ad-free. Overlap is the largest, and a kids' mode is a small change for it.
2. **Artie, medium.** Closest design to Sharp Ears and the model Roy followed. Raised under EUR 1M in its latest reported round, no kids' features on its page, Android unclear. Becomes high if it ships Android and a kids' mode. Release speed: Not found.
3. **Skoove, medium.** Free tier, mic listening, recommends itself for kids 6+, small funding (EUR 3M in 2019). Release speed: Not found.

## 5. Strategic recommendations

- **Position to own:** the practice companion for a family's one piano, kids first, on Android, starting in Canada.
- **Feature to ship first:** the kid world map with profiles and the parent-controlled mic explainer. It is the one differentiator a parent can understand on one screen.
- **Competitor to watch:** Simply Piano. It already has the profiles, the child adaptation and the Android audience, so it is the likeliest to enter this exact niche.
